Three simple instincts

A flock is a conversation.

Each bird listens only to its neighbors. The pattern belongs to everyone.

01 Give each other space.

Separation nudges birds away from close neighbors. Turn it up and watch the flock breathe.

02 Find a shared direction.

Alignment steers toward the average velocity of nearby birds. A little agreement becomes a flowing current.

03 Stay in the neighborhood.

Cohesion pulls birds toward their neighbors’ average position. It turns scattered individuals into a group.

The whole idea, in one linenext velocity = current velocity + separation + alignment + cohesionSmall changes. Every frame.
A little closer to the math

Each bird has a position and a velocity (a direction with a speed). It checks a circle 65 pixels around itself. Separation adds pushes away from birds within 25 pixels, weighted more strongly when they are closer. Alignment compares the neighbors’ average velocity to its own. Cohesion steers toward the neighbors’ average position.

The sliders multiply these three steering vectors. We add them, limit acceleration and speed, then update the position: position ← position + velocity × time. Your pointer adds another force. Orbital mode adds a gentle swirl around the center. The edges wrap, so a bird leaving one side returns on the other.

Turn on Show the math to follow the white bird. Its outer circle shows who it can see; the inner circle shows personal space. The three colored arrows show its weighted steering contributions: peach = separation, green = alignment, blue = cohesion. Arrow lengths share a visual scale. “In sync” measures how well the birds’ directions agree: 100% means everyone points the same way; near 0% means directions cancel out.

This is a Boids model, introduced by Craig Reynolds in 1986. It is an example of emergence: a complex group pattern growing from simple local decisions. Try setting all three instincts to zero, then bringing alignment back.
